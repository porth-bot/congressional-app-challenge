Findings

Phone calls carry less soundNetworks keep roughly 300 to 3,400 Hz and drop the rest.
Detectors are trained on studio audioOn real-world clips, error rates rose 200% to 1000%.3
Liveline keeps what survivesClean audio is run through phone codecs, and only features that still separate real and cloned voices are used.

Calculation

Example cutoffs are 30% and 70%. Real cutoffs will be tuned on validation data to keep false alarms on family calls rare. Signals are assumed roughly independent.

Components

VoiceSmall model trained only on features that survive phone audio. A mismatch with a family member’s voice sample weighs heavily.
LanguageStreaming speech-to-text and a language model that flags scam tactics. Independent of audio quality, so it also catches human scammers.
Safe wordUsed only in the yellow band. Whispering may be hard for real-time voice converters. This is a hypothesis we are testing.
StackCloud telephony with live audio streaming (such as Twilio), Python, FastAPI, librosa or openSMILE, scikit-learn or PyTorch.

Study 1: Phone-audio benchmark

Planned
DataASVspoof 2019 for training. In-the-Wild, 37.9 hours of real-world audio, for testing.3
Phone simulationEvery clip is run through G.711 and AMR-NB codecs to create matched clean and phone copies.
BaselinesA spectrogram CNN, plus a pretrained detector if one runs on our hardware.
MetricsEER and AUC, five-fold cross-validation, McNemar’s test.

Study 2: Senior pilot

Stretch goal
Participants10 to 15 volunteers aged 60 or older.
TaskSix short calls, half scams. Repeated with new calls and the Trust Meter on screen.
MeasuresScams caught, false alarms on real calls, ease of use from 1 to 5.
ConsentVoluntary, can stop at any time, no identifying data kept.
